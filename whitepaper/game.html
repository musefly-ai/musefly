Plain-text rules: muse.txt — the file your agent reads. Exam room: run it yourself.

You are not one fly, you are a bloodline trying to out-reproduce a wild-type rival across generations, under a predator that hunts by vision. No install, no account, no wallet — open /play and you are in. The lineage is saved in your own browser (localStorage key flyline_v1); nothing about the game depends on a chain.

Source of truth: the pure logic modules (sim.js) and the game loop (scene-game.js). The escape circuit itself is specified in paper 5.

1 · The loop

forage → energy → eggs (automatic) → generation ends (≤ 50 s)
   → compare eggs with the wild type → draft 1 of 3 mutations
   → next generation inherits it → the wild type evolves too

A generation ends three ways: energy hits zero, the predator kills you, or you survive the full 50 seconds. Death ends a generation, not the lineage.

2 · Food: three risk levels

The richest food is the most dangerous. Eating strategy is a positioning decision, not a menu: what matters is where the food leaves you when the strike comes.

3 · The predator

A state machine with three phases:

approach (steers to nearest/smelliest fly)
   → lunge (ballistic, committed trajectory, ~0.45 s, triggers slow-motion)
   → recover (0.5 s) → approach

The lunge is committed: once it fires, the trajectory does not track you. This is what makes the GF reflex readable — watch the approach, wait for READY, jump clear of a line you can predict. The predator gets faster every generation and at night; smelly flies (rot eaters, post-laying) get hunted first.

4 · The escape moment

  • The HUD shows LC4 and LPLC2 activations, the GF membrane potential, and a READY state (the circuit is paper 5).
  • When READY lights up: Space (desktop) or the GF ESCAPE button (touch).
  • A reflex escape costs 60% of a manual dash, grants ~0.18 s of invulnerability, and fires away from the predator automatically — the circuit aims, you time it.
  • Jumping before READY is possible (the hopper trait) but costs more and recoils shorter.

The tensest seconds of the game are the ones where you choose not to jump yet.

Several traits have designed synergies — tiger + nocturnal night scavenging, swift + shaker chain-escape, fecund + forager egg engine. Builds are real; so are the trade-offs: every gain has a cost printed on the card.

6 · The arms race

The wild type is not static. Every generation it drafts one mutation, deterministically from the same island number — so a lineage that ignores it faces an increasingly specialized rival. The end-of-generation screen compares your eggs against its eggs; the long game is lineage eggs and best, both kept in your local save.

7 · Reproducibility

  • The island number (seed) — the whole world (food layout, rival draft, predator spawn) derives from one seed, editable in the menu. Same island, same world.
  • Connectivity toggle — switch the escape circuit between real and swapped wiring and feel the difference in READY timing (the wiring check says it is ~0.02 s of lead time; the game lets you try to notice it).
  • Agent drive — window.FlyLabAPI exposes getState(), setControl({x,y}), dash() and setMode("manual"|"auto"|"agent"), so an autonomous process can play the same loop with the same rules (paper 10).

The full engineering record of cross-machine determinism is paper 6. Share your island number and anyone can re-run your fly's whole life — which is what turns "my AI survived" from a boast into a checkable claim.

9 · What the game is not

  • It is not a chain game: no wallet, no mint, no transaction ever appears in /play. The mint lives behind a separate, server-verified lane (paper 8).
  • It is not a claim about fly biology. The traits reference real mutations and behaviors (white, Adh, rover/sitter, diapause…), tuned for play, not for literature accuracy.
  • Local saves are local. They are not credentials, not missions, and never count toward any server-side qualification.
